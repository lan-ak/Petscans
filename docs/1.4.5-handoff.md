## 1. What's in the build

Version **1.4.5**, build **13**. Archive and signed IPA at
`.asc/artifacts/PetScans-1.4.5.{xcarchive,ipa}` (25.4 MB).

Three app changes, all of them required by the screenshot work rather than chosen
alongside it.

### The hero shows its score
`RatingLabelView` takes an optional `score` and renders it above the word:
**92/100** over *Excellent*. The number was already computed and already stored —
History has been rendering `Int(scan.totalScore)` all along — it just never
appeared on the screen the shot is taken from.

The number is suppressed when the word and the number disagree. `ratingLabel` is
not a pure function of `total`: a suitability explanation can carry a
`labelOverride` that drags the word down to *Avoid* while the total stays high,
and a badge reading "82/100" over "Avoid" reads as a rendering bug rather than as
the warning it is. `ProductScoreView.heroScore` gates on that, and on
`scoresAreMissing`, where a stored zero means absence rather than a score of zero.

**Worth knowing before this ships:** putting the number on screen makes the
scoring distribution visible in a way the word never did. The catalog work has
99.7% of products scoring above 75, so most bags will now show a number in the
high eighties and nineties rather than the word "Excellent" twice. That is the
same problem as before, now legible.

### Saved scans show their allergens — this was a live bug
`allergenAlertBanner` was gated on `petName`, which a saved scan never carries
(`SavedScanDetailView` passes `nil`, because a `Scan` does not persist which pet
it was run for). `warningsSection` deliberately skips allergen flags with the
comment "allergen warnings are shown in the hero banner". So on every scan opened
from History, both were true and **every allergen the app had found rendered
nowhere**. A user who scanned a food that conflicted with their pet's profile and
came back to it later saw no warning at all.

The banner now renders whenever there are allergen flags. `AllergenAlertBanner`
takes `petName: String?` and drops the name from its headline when it has none —
"Contains Ingredients To Avoid" rather than "Contains Ingredients Luna Should
Avoid". The individual flag cards inside it already name the pet, because their
text is written at scan time when the pet is known.

This was found while shooting slot 3, whose whole subject is that banner.

### Screenshot seed data
`ScreenshotDataSeeder` gained six more real catalog rows (ORIJEN, ACANA, Natural
Balance, Blue Buffalo, Merrick, Solid Gold) so the History list has depth, and
now assigns explicit descending `scannedAt` values.

The timestamps are not cosmetic. Every row was stamped `Date()` inside `Scan.init`
during one loop, so nine rows shared a timestamp to the millisecond and History's
`sort: \Scan.scannedAt, order: .reverse` returned them in whatever order SwiftData
felt like. That put the hero product below the fold, and shot 1 — which opens a
scan by matching its name — silently opened a different Merrick product scoring
46 instead of the 92 it is supposed to show.

Every `imageUrl` in the seeder is now written from the catalog row for its GTIN
rather than transcribed.

## 2. Screenshots

Regenerate with `./Scripts/run_screenshots.sh`. Two sizes, 6.9" (1320×2868, iPhone
17 Pro Max) and 6.5" (1284×2778, iPhone 14 Plus). Upload from the `*-framed`
folders. Output is RGB at 72 dpi with no alpha, which is what App Store Connect
accepts.

The framing changed. Previously each shot was a whole device screenshot pasted on
green — status bar, navigation bar and tab bar included, roughly a fifth of the
canvas spent on chrome. Four rules now govern every frame, and all four exist
because breaking them produced something that looked broken rather than composed.

**No chrome.** The status bar, the navigation bar and the tab bar are cropped
before the shot is placed. A back chevron, a "Scan Details" title and a
share/star pill are not the product — and the blurred backdrop material behind a
navigation bar renders as grey smudging once it is sitting on a green marketing
frame, with whatever was scrolled underneath ghosting through it. A 1.8% shave
off each side takes the scroll indicator with it.

**One card is lifted out.** Each shot names a single card, which is cropped from
the raw capture, enlarged, shadowed, and floated *over the row it came from* — so
it reads as a lift rather than as a duplicate of something still visible below.
Where the card sits is recorded by the tests, not guessed: `ScreenshotTests`
writes each float's frame, normalised to the window, into `<shot>.floats.json`
beside the PNG, so one set of coordinates serves both device sizes.
`saveFloatRects` fails the test if the frame is off screen.

**Cuts land between elements, never through them.** Both the float's edges and
the panel's bottom are snapped to the nearest gutter. The measure is not "quietest
row" — the flat middle of a warning card is exactly as quiet as the gap between
two cards, and a quietest-row search happily put a sliver of the Avoid badge at
the bottom of shot 3 and cut Ingredient Recognition in half on shot 2. It samples
the page colour from the left gutter, where cards do not reach, and looks for rows
that are almost entirely that colour. The bottom cut may travel a long way up to
reach one, because moving up only trims content; it may barely move down, because
down is where the tab bar is.

**Whatever is left over is shared.** Snapping the cut upward leaves the panel
shorter than the space it was sized for, so the slack is split between the caption
and the bottom margin rather than dumped underneath. The panel sits balanced in
the frame instead of looking like it ran out.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scan the bag. Get a score. | the rating badge, now **92/100** | recut + the number |
| 2 | See what's actually harmful | BHA/BHT, with its FDA citation | recut |

affiliate, sponsorship, or commission path anywhere in the app. The only retailer
domains present are a filter list in `SerperService`.

## 3. Metadata

`metadata/version/1.4.5/en-US.json`. The description is four sections and no
feature bullets, following what Yuka's listing does — catalog size, how a food is
scored, who pays us (nobody), where the data comes from. The eight-bullet KEY
FEATURES list is gone. iOS does not index the description; it is conversion copy,
and eight bullets read as a spec sheet.

Keywords are unchanged from 1.4.4 (97/100 chars, already correct).

### Store languages

Eleven new locales, listing only — no strings inside the app change, which is why
this needs no build:

`de-DE`, `es-ES`, `es-MX`, `fr-CA`, `fr-FR`, `it`, `ja`, `ko`, `nl-NL`, `pt-BR`,
`zh-Hans`

Each carries a localised name, subtitle, keywords, description, promotional text
and release notes. `asc metadata validate --dir ./metadata` reports 28 files, 0
issues. Every field is under its character limit; the tightest are the German name
at 28/30 and the Portuguese keywords at 99/100.

### Territories

Currently available in **5 of 175** territories (US, CA, GB, AU, NZ) — the July
audit figure is still accurate, confirmed against
`asc pricing availability territory-availabilities`.

The command is in §5 and is deliberately sequenced **after** submission. Running
it now would publish the *current* listing — 1.4.4's screenshots, English only —
to 170 new storefronts, because new localisations and new screenshots only go live
with the version that carries them. Expanding first delivers the old listing
worldwide and then updates it later, which is the wrong way round.
