# CREATOR AGREEMENT

**PetScans Creator Program**

---

Between **Akinyemi & Co Ltd**, an Ontario corporation of [REGISTERED ADDRESS], which makes the **PetScans** app (**"Company"**), and [CREATOR LEGAL NAME] of [CREATOR ADDRESS] (**"Creator"**), starting [EFFECTIVE DATE].

## 1. The Deal

1.1 **What this is.** Creator makes short videos promoting the app, posts them, and keeps them up. Company pays per video, plus bonuses when a video does well (Exhibit A). Neither side is locked in: Company may work with other creators and owes Creator no minimum number of months.

1.2 **Creator confirms** they are 18 or over, live in and may lawfully work in the United States, own or care for a dog or cat, and control accounts in good standing on each platform in clause 4.1.

1.3 **Creator is a contractor, not an employee** — no benefits, no authority to bind Company, and Creator decides how, when, and where the work gets done. Company's scripts and feedback aim at the result, not the method. Creator pays their own taxes, including self-employment tax, and provides a completed IRS Form W-9 before the first payment. Company is Canadian and will not withhold.

1.4 **Exclusivity.** While this Agreement runs, and for **30 days** after, Creator will not make paid content for any app or service whose main job is scanning, rating, or scoring pet food. Company has not named one as at the start date, and can name one later in writing, effective from then. The rest of the pet category — toys, insurance, grooming, food brands themselves — stays open, so long as it does not run within 7 days of a video for Company in a way that confuses viewers about who is sponsoring what.

## 2. Trial, Term, and Ending It

2.1 **The first 5 days are a trial.** Either side can walk away during them, at any time, for any reason or none, effective immediately. Videos already approved and posted still get paid and still earn bonuses. Nothing else is owed either way — no minimum, no penalty. Clauses 4.3 and 8 still cover anything already posted, and the exclusivity in clause 1.4 ends the day this does.

2.2 **Rolling monthly, and ending it later.** This runs month to month, renewing automatically, and the trial is part of the first month. After the trial, either side ends this on **7 days'** written notice. Clauses 4.3 and 8 still cover anything already posted.

2.3 **Ending it for cause.** Company can end this immediately, paying only for videos already accepted, if Creator breaches clause 6 (disclosure), 7.4 (fake views), or 1.4 (exclusivity); breaches clause 5 in a way clause 5.6 allows this for; is banned by a platform in a way that stops delivery; does something that reasonably brings Company or the app into disrepute; or breaches anything else and does not fix it within 5 business days of notice.

2.4 **What survives.** Clauses 1.3, 4.3, 4.4, and 5 to 11 carry on after this Agreement ends. Unpaid fees for accepted videos stay payable.

## 3. What Creator Makes

3.1 **One video per day**, around 30 a month. Company provides scripts, concepts, reference videos, and feedback free of charge, and Creator can pitch their own ideas too.

3.2 **Each video must be:**

|   |   |
|---|---|
| Length | 15–30 seconds |
| Format | Vertical 9:16, at least 1080 × 1920 |
| Audio | Clear speech or captions. Any music must be licensed for paid ads — platform audio libraries usually are not. |
| Who's in it | Creator and/or Creator's own dog or cat |
| Original | Made new for this Agreement, not posted before |

3.3 **Raw files.** If Company asks, Creator sends the source files within 3 business days, over Discord or a transfer link if they are too big.

## 4. Posting

4.1 **Every video goes on all five** — **TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, and Snapchat** — on the accounts listed in Exhibit B, whether those are Creator's own or set up for this program and owned by Company (clause 8.6). All five within **24 hours** of the first, each carrying the caption, hashtags, disclosure (clause 6), and link Company specifies.

4.2 **Review before posting.** Creator submits each video on Company's Discord. Company replies within **2 business days** — silence means approved. Two rounds of revisions are included; past that, or if Company changes the concept after approving it, the two sides agree a fee or Company drops the concept. A rejected video Creator chooses not to fix earns nothing. Company approving a video does not let Creator off clauses 5, 6, or 9.1.

4.3 **Keep posts up for 12 months** from posting, including after this Agreement ends — public, not archived, not private, disclosure intact. Creator can take one down sooner if the law, a platform, or a real safety concern requires it, telling Company promptly.

4.4 **Reporting.** Within 3 days of each month ending, Creator sends every live URL plus analytics screenshots showing views per platform. Company can ask for updated numbers any time.

## 5. Content Rules

5.1 **Creator's voice is Creator's own.** Company's scripts are starting points, not a required read — rewrite them, improvise, or ignore them. Clauses 5.2 to 5.5 are the limits on what Creator says; clause 6 covers disclosure.

5.2 **Say only what is true.** Do not claim to use, like, or benefit from the app unless that is true when posting, and show only results Creator actually got. What Creator genuinely noticed in their own pet is fair game. Anything said about pricing, subscription terms, or features has to be accurate — public information is fine, but offers, discount codes, and unreleased features need Company's written go-ahead.

5.3 **No disease claims.** Do not say or imply that the app, or any food or ingredient, diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents an illness in an animal, and do not pass content off as veterinary advice. Personal observations — coat, energy, appetite — are fine. Telling viewers what will happen to their animal is not.

5.4 **Naming a brand.** Creator **may** name or show a specific pet food brand, including critically, once Company approves that brand and that claim in writing — Company holds the substantiation. Without approval, do not identify a brand or product, by name or packaging, while calling it unsafe, deceptive, or the cause of an animal's illness. Ingredients, categories, and label practices in general need no approval, and neither does showing food Creator actually feeds.

5.5 **Lawful and licensed.** Nothing unlawful, defamatory, harassing, hateful, or sexually explicit. Nothing showing animal cruelty, neglect, or unsafe handling. No minors without a parent's consent. No footage, music, or likenesses Creator is not licensed to use. Follow each platform's rules.

5.6 **Fix first.** If a video breaks these rules but can be fixed, Company asks Creator to edit, re-post, or remove it before ending anything. Immediate termination is for a repeat after that request, or a breach of clause 5.3 or 5.4 that lands Company with a claim.

## 6. Disclosure

6.1 **Every video says it is an ad.** Creator is paid, so the FTC requires this be disclosed (16 CFR Part 255) — in **every** video, and in any other post, story, comment, or stream mentioning the app. For each video Creator will:

- turn on the platform's paid-partnership tool, where it has one;

- put **#ad** or **#sponsored** at the **start** of the caption, above the “more” cut-off, not buried in hashtags; and

- add an on-screen or spoken disclosure in the first 3 seconds, where the format allows.

Disclosures go in plain language, in the language of the video, readable against whatever is behind them.

6.2 **Leave it there** for the 12 months in clause 4.3. If Company flags a post, Creator fixes or removes it within 24 hours, and bonuses on that video pause until they do.

## 7. Money

7.1 **Rates are in Exhibit A.** Company pays monthly, within **15 days** of each month ending, by [PAYMENT METHOD], in **US dollars**. Creator covers processor and conversion fees on their side, and their own production costs unless Company approves an expense in writing first. A bonus is paid in the first cycle after it is confirmed, even where the video went up months earlier. Exhibit A is the whole of what Creator gets, including for every right assigned or granted in clause 8 and all future use of the videos in ads.

7.2 **Counting views.** Views come from each platform's own analytics on Creator's posts, shown by screenshot or view-only access, and count for up to **90 days** after posting. A bonus is locked in the moment the number is first hit; a later drop does not take it back. If the two sides' numbers disagree, Company works it through with Creator in good faith before deciding.

7.3 **After this ends.** Bonuses on videos posted beforehand still pay if the number lands inside the 90 days — including after a trial cancellation — unless Company ended things under clause 2.3.

7.4 **Views have to be earned.** No bots, click farms, engagement pods, or bought views, and no boosting without Company's written approval. **Paid views never count toward a bonus** — whether Creator boosted the post or Company ran ads through it under clause 8.3. Where Company advertises on a video, bonuses run off the platform's organic figure; if the platform does not separate organic from paid, the count for that video freezes at where it stood the day before Company's first ad ran. If Company reasonably concludes a bonus came from bought or bot views, Creator repays it within 15 days, and Company can set it against anything else owed.

7.5 **Final payment.** Within 30 days of this ending, Company sends a final statement: base fees for accepted videos not yet paid, plus bonuses already earned, less anything Creator owes. Company can hold back a fair amount against a video not yet up on all five platforms, a disclosure not yet fixed under clause 6.2, or an account or login not yet handed back under clause 8.6 — and releases it once Creator sorts that out. Bonuses that land after the final statement are paid in the next monthly cycle.

## 8. Rights in the Videos

8.1 **Company owns the videos.** As each video is made, Creator assigns to Company all copyright and other intellectual property in it, worldwide, for the full life of those rights. Company may use, copy, share, display, edit, adapt, translate, excerpt, and build on them, whole or in part, in any medium. That covers:

- organic and paid social anywhere, including dark posts, TikTok Spark Ads, and Meta and Instagram Partnership Ads;

- Company's websites, app store listings, in-app screens, email, display, connected TV, and out-of-home; and

- press, investor, and internal material, and recuts mixing Creator's videos with other creators' work.

Creator will sign anything Company reasonably needs to record or perfect the assignment, at Company's cost. If a right cannot be assigned, Creator grants Company the widest exclusive licence over it that the law allows instead. If Company fails to pay for a video within 30 days of it falling due and does not put that right within 10 days of written notice, the rights in that video revert to Creator.

8.2 **Creator's licence back.** Company licenses each video back to Creator, perpetually and free, to keep it posted on Creator's own accounts as clause 4.3 requires, and to show it in Creator's portfolio, reel, and pitches as an example of their work. Creator will not license the videos to anyone else or run them as ads for anyone else.

8.3 **Ad codes.** When Company asks, Creator issues TikTok Spark Ad and Meta Partnership Ad codes for any video, for the longest the platform allows, and renews them on request. Creator will not pull a code while Company is running ads on it.

8.4 **Name and likeness.** Company may use Creator's name, handle, voice, image, and likeness, and their pet's name and likeness, for those same purposes. As far as the law allows, Creator waives moral rights in the videos against Company and anyone Company licenses them to.

8.5 **Company's side.** Scripts, briefs, brand assets, trademarks, and the app stay Company's, and Creator uses them only for this work. If a video's audio is not cleared for paid ads, Creator flags it and Company swaps it.

8.6 **Accounts.** Accounts Creator already had stay Creator's. Any account set up for this program at Company's request is **Company's** — the handle, the login, and the following — and Creator uses it only for this work. Creator keeps Company's accounts on logins and recovery details Company can access at all times, does not change them, and hands everything over when Company asks or when this ends, posting nothing further. Where a platform will not recognise a transfer, Creator holds the account for Company and does what Company reasonably asks with it. Clause 6 applies to Company's accounts exactly as it does to Creator's.

## 9. Promises and Cover

9.1 **Creator promises** they are free to sign this and assign the rights in clause 8; the videos are their own original work, infringe nobody, and they hold written releases from anyone else recognisable in them; what they say is true and meets clauses 5 and 6 and the law, including the FTC Act; and the analytics they send are unaltered.

9.2 **Creator covers Company** against third-party claims, and the resulting losses and reasonable legal fees, arising from a breach of clause 5, 6, 7.4, 8, or 9.1, from a claim that a video infringes someone's rights, or from Creator's negligence or deliberate wrongdoing.

9.3 **Company covers Creator** against third-party claims that Company's own brand assets, trademarks, or approved scripts infringe someone's intellectual property, where Creator used them as supplied. Whoever wants cover gives prompt notice; the other side runs the defence, and cannot settle in a way that binds them to something uncovered without their consent.

9.4 **Limits.** Apart from those two promises and a breach of clause 8, neither side is liable for indirect, incidental, consequential, or punitive damages, or lost profits. Company's total liability is capped at what it paid or owes Creator over the 3 months before whatever went wrong.

## 10. Confidentiality

10.1 **Keep it to yourself.** Creator keeps Company's non-public information to themselves for **3 years** after this ends, and uses it only for this work: unreleased features, roadmaps, campaign strategy, performance data, what other creators are paid, and these terms. It does not cover anything already public through no fault of Creator's, anything Creator already knew, or anything they work out independently.

10.2 **What Creator can still say.** None of that stops Creator disclosing the paid relationship as clause 6 and the law require, answering a government inquiry truthfully, discussing their own pay where the law protects that, or complying with a legal order — telling Company first where they are allowed to.

## 11. Everything Else

11.1 **Ontario law** governs, and the **courts of Ontario in Toronto** have exclusive jurisdiction, after 30 days of trying to sort it out in good faith. Either side can still ask any competent court for an injunction to protect intellectual property or confidential information.

11.2 **Discord runs the day-to-day** — briefs, submissions, approvals, feedback, reporting. Creator joins the server, stays in it, keeps notifications on for the channels Company names, and replies within 2 business days. **Anything legal — termination, breach, claims — goes by email** to the addresses below and takes effect on confirmed delivery. A Discord message is not formal notice, losing Discord access does not extend a deadline, and since Company can moderate or delete Discord content, Creator keeps their own copies of videos and approvals.

11.3 **This is the whole agreement**, replacing anything said beforehand, including the recruiting post. Changes must be written and signed by both sides — though Company can update briefs, scripts, and caption requirements any time, which are not changes to this Agreement.

11.4 **Transfers.** Creator needs Company's written consent to hand this Agreement, or any part of the work, to someone else. Company can transfer it to an affiliate, or on a sale or merger of the business.

11.5 **Odds and ends.** A waiver only counts in writing. An unenforceable provision gets narrowed just enough to work, and the rest stands. This can be signed in counterparts and electronically — e-signatures count the same as ink under Ontario's Electronic Commerce Act, 2000 and the U.S. ESIGN Act.

## Exhibit A — What Creator Gets Paid

### Base

**US $15 per approved video**, once it is up on all five platforms. Posted to fewer than five, it earns nothing until the rest go up. At one a day, that is about **$450 a month**.

### Bonuses

| When one video hits | It earns | That video's total |
|---|---|---|
| 100,000 views | + $100 | $115 |
| 1,000,000 views | + $300 more | $415 |

**Views** means what one video gets across all five platforms **added together**, on Creator's own accounts, within 90 days of posting (clause 7.2). Paid and bought views do not count, including views from Company's own ads (clause 7.4).

Each milestone pays once per video, and the $300 sits on top of the $100. No cap per month.

### For example

Thirty approved videos in a month, four of them past 100,000 views, one of those four going on past a million:

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 30 × $15 | $450 |
| 100k bonus | 4 × $100 | $400 |
| 1M bonus | 1 × $300 | $300 |
| Month total |   | $1,150 |
