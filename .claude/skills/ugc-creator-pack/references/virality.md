# Virality is a feature you build, not an outcome you hope for

Every script ships with **named mechanics** that cause spread. If you cannot list which
mechanics a script carries and what each one is supposed to make the viewer *do*, the script
is not finished — it's a nice video with no distribution plan.

You cannot guarantee a hit. You can guarantee that a script contains the things hits contain,
and that you'll know within 48 hours whether it worked. That's the difference between a
feature and a guess.

## What the data says about this category

From `PetScans UGC/pet-food-scanner-tiktok-landscape.md` — 363 videos:

- **244 of 363 landed at `organic_hit`** (>5% engagement). This topic *wants* to travel.
- **The topic carries the video, not the audience.** 8.3K followers → 6.77M views
  (**BR 11,402×**). 1,114 followers → 2.13M (BR 2,629×). 242 followers → 601K (BR 1,049×).
  A small creator is not a constraint here; it's the normal case for a breakout.
- **Every scanner app that "won" bought it.** The category's biggest app videos are
  `likely_paid` — 100,690 views on **98 likes** (~0.01%). Views without engagement is the
  failure mode we are specifically avoiding, and it's what the whole category has settled for.

So: **judge by engagement rate and breakout ratio, never raw views.** A 100K-view video at
0.01% is a worse outcome than a 20K-view video at 8%.

## The seven mechanics

Each script must carry **at least three**, and **mechanic 1 is mandatory in every script.**

### 1. The verification invitation (mandatory)

End on an action the viewer can complete in 30 seconds with something they already own:
*"Go check the bag in your cupboard. I'll wait."*

This is the engine. It converts watching into doing, and doing into commenting — and the
comment is almost always a score, which seeds the next viewer's curiosity. It is also,
conveniently, the product demo. No other mechanic does three jobs at once.

Write it as an instruction, not an invitation to consider. "Go turn yours over" beats "you
might want to check yours."

### 2. The argument engine

Name a brand people already defend. The research logged a **1.31M-view defence** of Purina
alongside a **2.13M-view attack** — the fight itself is the distribution.

Choose products with two existing sides, then **be scrupulously fair on camera** so we're the
referee and not a combatant. The "none of this is poison, it's just unnecessary" beat is not
softness — it's what makes the defenders argue in the comments instead of reporting the video.
Tell the creator: answer with the ingredient list, never an opinion, and never delete.

### 3. The pause frame

Give the ingredient panel real time on screen and **hold it still** — at least 2 seconds
without scrolling. Pausing inflates watch time, and a paused frame gets screenshotted and
reposted somewhere we have no presence.

Design the frame to be worth pausing on: a numbered list, a stack of warnings, a rank the
viewer will want to count themselves. A still that survives being screenshotted does more for
distribution than any amount of scrolling UI. (Branding is the opposite — the splash or logo
card is capped at 2 seconds; see the framing rule in `SKILL.md`.)

### 4. The stitch bait

Pose a question to camera that another creator can answer in their own video:
*"This bag is called Lamb Flavor. Tell me when we hit the lamb."*

Stitches and duets are free distribution from someone else's audience. Any script built around
a countable list or a challenge to the viewer is stitch bait; make it explicit rather than
accidental.

### 5. The tag trigger

Name a specific identity so viewers tag someone: *"If you've got more than one animal in the
house, this is the bit that'll get you."* People share to say something about themselves or
someone they know — not to transmit information. Give them the sentence.

### 6. The screenshot artifact

One frame that carries the whole story with the sound off: the red **0/100 · AVOID** beside
the bag. It has to be legible as a still, because that still is what gets reposted to other
platforms where we have no presence.

### 7. The re-skin — across time, never across creators

When a hook shape works, repeat it on a new product instead of inventing a new one. Virality
compounds through repetition of a proven shape; novelty for its own sake throws away
everything you learned.

**But re-skin along one axis only: time and product, within a single creator's own feed.**

**Never give two creators the same hook shape in the same window.** Undisclosed posts running
a matched template across several accounts is exactly the fingerprint that identifies a paid
seed network — it is how we identified a competitor's network in
`PetScans UGC/competitor-scanner-apps.md`, where ~10 low-follower accounts all ran *"Most
TOXIC [dog/cat] food you NEED to AVOID"*. Two of ours doing the same thing is detectable by
anyone who looks, and the accusation is unanswerable.

So:

- **Every creator gets hook shapes nobody else is running.** Check `PetScans UGC/hook-ledger.md`
  before writing, and add yours when the pack ships.
- **Stagger releases** — different creators' packs shouldn't land in the same week.
- **Same product across creators is fine.** Same *hook shape* is not. Two people can both
  scan Meow Mix; they can't both open with "you bought the salmon one."
- Re-skin freely *within* one creator: same shape, next product, weeks apart. That's the
  compounding this mechanic is for.

## Share, comment, save are different jobs

Design for the one you want; they don't come as a bundle.

| Goal | What causes it |
|---|---|
| **Comment** | An answerable question, a score to report, or something to argue with |
| **Share** | Identity — "this is about you / us" — not usefulness |
| **Save** | A list or reference the viewer intends to act on later (aisle walkthroughs, tier rankings) |
| **Follow** | A promise of more of the same series, stated |

## Deliberately seed the top comment

Leave one obvious question unanswered — the brand you *didn't* scan, the score you didn't
explain. A predictable top comment is one you can reply to fast, and an early reply thread is
the cheapest distribution there is. Note the intended seed in the internal `.md` so whoever
runs the account is ready for it.

## Measure it

Record the prediction before publishing, then check it. Without this it's a guess again.

- **Get the creator's median views** at intake. Without it you cannot compute breakout ratio,
  and BR is the only number that separates "the content worked" from "they have an audience."
  A media kit with no stats blocks measurement — see `creator-intake.md`.
- **Target: BR ≥ 3× and engagement ≥ 5%** (`organic_hit`). Below 3% engagement, the creative
  failed regardless of views.
- **48-hour read.** Scale the winners by re-skinning the hook shape (mechanic 7) onto the next
  product; kill the rest. Do not iterate a script that came in under 3% — replace the driver.
- **Never celebrate raw views.** That's the mistake the whole competitor set has made.

## What you cannot control

Timing, algorithm weather, and whether a bigger account happens to be posting the same week.
Don't pretend otherwise, and don't let a single flat video kill a hook shape that tested well
elsewhere. Judge shapes across several posts, not one.
