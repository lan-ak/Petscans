# Emotional hooks

The hook is the whole video's ceiling. Everything downstream — the label read, the score
flip — only gets watched if the first 5 seconds land. This is the part that must stay
consistent across every pack, every creator, every species.

## The rule

**One driver per script. Never two.** A hook that tries to be both frightening and heartwarming
is neither. Pick the driver first, write the line second.

**And the five scripts in a pack must span different drivers.** Five creatives running in one
ad account with the same emotional register cannibalise each other — you learn nothing from
the test because they're all the same ad wearing different products.

## The six drivers

| Driver | What it sounds like | Use for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Guilt / love** | "If you love your dog, watch this before you feed them tonight." | Allergy storytime; anything where the owner has been unknowingly doing harm | Tips into shaming fast. Aim at the *bag*, never the viewer. |
| **Betrayal / distrust** | "The bag says lamb. So where's the lamb?" | Front-label vs ingredient-panel mismatch | The strongest and safest driver we have. Needs a verifiable panel. |
| **Fear** | "She was tearing her own skin open and I was feeding her the thing doing it." | **Allergens above all** — also recalls and genuinely toxic-for-species ingredients | Fear about *what an allergen did to their animal* is theirs to tell, and it's the most powerful register we have. Fear about *what the app said* must match the screen. |
| **Relief / transformation** | "I haven't cleaned a carpet at 4am in two months." | Before/after, the payoff end of a storytime | Must be real. Never script a health outcome the creator didn't have. |
| **Authority** | "As a clinical pet nutritionist, here's what I wouldn't buy." | Vets, nutritionists, rescue operators | Only if the credential is real. |
| **Urgency** | "Check your bag against this recall code." | Time-sensitive news | We don't control recall timing; opportunistic only. |

### Fear, specifically

**Lead with it on allergen scripts.** It is the strongest driver in this category and we have
the only product that speaks to it. An allergic animal is genuinely miserable — skin torn
open, chronic ear infections, years of vomiting — and the owner watching it happen is
frightened and guilty at the same time. Let the creator say that with force. The highest
breakout in the entire landscape study is an allergy video (6.77M, BR 11,402×).

The line to hold is not intensity, it's **subject**:

- **Their animal, their experience** — fear as strong as they actually felt it. No hedging,
  no clinical register. "Three years. Three years of this and it was in the bag the whole time."
- **What the app said** — must match the screen exactly. If it reads Caution, the creator does
  not get to call the product toxic. That's not a tone limit, it's a factual one.

So: terrified about what happened to your dog, precise about what the app reported.

Two secondary drivers worth naming because they carry a lot of our best hooks:

- **Curiosity gap** — "I found a dog food with six separate warnings on it. Six." Withholds the
  answer. Pairs with a hard number.
- **Late discovery** — "Two years. Two years I thought this was just what she did." The viewer
  recognises themselves in a mistake that already happened.

## Which driver goes in which slot

The five-slot structure from `script-craft.md`, with the driver each slot exists to carry:

| Slot | Script job | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Front-label betrayal — "you bought the salmon/lamb one" | **Betrayal** + late discovery |
| 2 | Count the warnings | **Curiosity gap** |
| 3 | **The allergy story** — what it did to their animal, and how long they didn't know | **Fear** |
| 4 | The surprising ingredient, or "Where's the [protein]?" | **Guilt/love** or **surprise** |
| 5 | Dual-score hero / in-store | **Relief** — this is the one that ends well |

**Five slots, five different drivers.** If two scripts land on the same driver, change one —
that's the rule this table exists to satisfy, and it's the easiest one to break by accident
(betrayal will happily eat slots 1 *and* 4 if you let it).

**Slot 3 is the highest-ceiling script in the pack and the most often written badly.** Allergy
storytime is the top-performing format in the whole landscape study, it's the only one that
maps directly onto our per-pet differentiator, and it's where the creator has the most real
material. Do not hedge it into blandness — see *Fear, specifically* above. It should be the
one script that makes someone uncomfortable.

**Slot 5 must land positive.** Four scripts of "everything is bad" with no resolution reads as
a rant and converts badly; the pack needs one video where the tool *solves* something.

"Where's the [protein]?" is a betrayal variant. It's a strong script, but it competes with
slot 1 for the same driver — run it *as* slot 1 on a different product, or put it in slot 4
only when slot 1 is doing something else.

## How to build the line

Seven rules, all visible in the shipped packs:

1. **No product, no app, no brand in the first 5 seconds.** The app is the payoff, not the
   premise. Phone face-down or out of frame.
2. **Second person.** "You've done what I did. You stood in the aisle and you picked the
   salmon one." The viewer is in the story before they've decided to watch it.
3. **Universalise so it isn't an accusation.** "*Everybody* buys the salmon one." The mistake
   is the category's fault, not theirs — that's what keeps guilt from becoming shame.
4. **A hard number or a timeframe.** "Six." / "Two years." / "Three years of 4am." Specificity
   reads as true and gives the edit an on-screen text stamp.
5. **Withhold the app until after the label read.** The order is problem → evidence → tool.
   Leading with the tool makes it an ad.
6. **End the hook unresolved.** The first beat asks a question the next 20 seconds answer.
7. **It has to be true for this creator.** A rescue hook in a farm creator's mouth is dead on
   arrival, and vice versa. Check it against `references/creator-intake.md`.

**Note that no shape appears twice across the two packs.** Both slot 1s are betrayal +
late discovery, but they're built completely differently — Ellie's is *"you bought the
[protein] one,"* Kara's is *"everything here eats what it should, except the dog."* Same
driver, same job, different shape.

That is a hard rule, not a preference. Posts go out undisclosed, and a matched template
across accounts is the fingerprint of a paid seed network — see mechanic 7 in
`references/virality.md`. **Claim your shapes in `PetScans UGC/hook-ledger.md` before you
write, and add them when the pack ships.** The ledger also lists unclaimed shapes to start
from.

## Anti-patterns

- **Two drivers in one hook.** "This is terrifying and also it healed my dog" — pick one.
- **Attributing fear to the app when the app didn't say it.** Being frightened by what an
  ingredient did to your animal: fine, and encouraged. Saying "the app says this is toxic"
  when the screen reads Caution: not fine. The correction outperforms the claim.
- **Medical claims.** No diagnosis, no treatment advice, no "this cures / this causes." The
  creator describes what happened to their animal, not what will happen to the viewer's.
- **Shaming the viewer.** "If you feed this you don't care about your dog." Always aim at the
  manufacturer.
- **Opening on the app.** "This app scans pet food!" is an ad, and it's scrolled past.
- **A hook the creator can't say truthfully.** If the driver needs a symptom or a history
  they don't have, change the driver, not the truth.
